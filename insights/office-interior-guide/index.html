Workplace

An office that works as hard as you do

Offices balance focus, meeting and social space. Acoustics and daylight are handled from day one.

Category
Workplace
Read time
5 min read
Studio
Woodex Studio

Zoning: focus, meet, social

Every office needs three kinds of space. Focus zones for deep work, meeting zones for collaboration, and social zones where the team actually talks.

Getting the ratio right matters more than any single finish. Watch how your team works for a week before the plan is drawn.

Acoustics and daylight

Open offices fail on noise, not looks. Acoustic panels, soft flooring and proper ceiling treatment keep conversations from carrying across the floor.

Daylight belongs near desks. Meeting rooms and services can sit deeper in the plan, where borrowed light does the job.

Brand cues

The office should feel like your company, not a catalogue. A restrained palette with one or two brand moments, done well, beats logos on every wall.

Reception carries the most weight. It is the first thing clients see and the last thing new hires remember from day one.

Plan for growth

Teams change. Modular workstations, movable partitions and spare data points let the layout flex without another full fit out.

For our full workplace service see office interior design.

The short version.

  • Three zones: Focus, meet and social space in the right ratio beats any finish.
  • Treat the noise: Acoustics decide whether an open office actually works.
  • Brand with restraint: One or two strong brand moments carry more than logos everywhere.

Questions

Asked often, answered plainly.

Plan 80 to 100 square feet per person including circulation and shared areas. Tighter than that and the office starts fighting itself.

A mix wins. Open focus zones with enough quiet rooms and small meeting spaces for calls and one to ones.

Acoustic ceiling treatment, panels on hard walls and soft flooring together. One measure alone rarely solves it.

Yes, and it usually lands better. Material and colour cues with one confident brand wall read as premium; repetition reads as decoration.
