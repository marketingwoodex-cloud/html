Office Layout Mistakes That Kill Productivity

Most office problems are not about style. They are layout decisions made too fast. Here are the mistakes we see most, and how to fix them before move-in.

Mistake 1: one big open floor with no quiet

The most common of all office design mistakes is an open plan with nowhere to concentrate or take a call. Noise spreads, people put on headphones and productivity drops. Add a few phone booths and a quiet zone, and separate loud teams from focus work.

Mistake 2: the wrong meeting room mix

Offices often build one large boardroom that sits empty while four people fight over a small room. Most meetings are two to four people. Plan more small rooms than large ones, and give each one screens and power.

Mistake 3: light, glare and air

  • Desks facing windows suffer screen glare.
  • Deep floors leave inner desks without daylight.
  • Cold spots under AC vents push staff to move desks.
  • Poor fresh air makes afternoons heavy.

Plan desk orientation, blinds and the AC layout together.

Getting the mix of spaces right

SpacePurpose
Open desksDay-to-day work and quick collaboration
Focus rooms or boothsCalls and concentrated work
Meeting roomsPlanned discussions and client meetings
Breakout areaBreaks, informal chats and lunch
StorageFiles, equipment and personal items

Most productivity complaints come from a missing space type, usually quiet rooms for calls.

Light, air and comfort

  • Place desks near daylight, at right angles to windows to avoid glare.
  • Provide fresh air, not only recirculated AC.
  • Keep temperature even across zones.
  • Use acoustic panels and soft finishes to reduce noise.

Planning for hybrid work

If staff work from home some days, not everyone needs a fixed desk. Shared desks, more meeting space and good video-call rooms often serve a hybrid team better than rows of empty workstations.

Before you sign a new office lease

  • Check the floor plate shape; deep, narrow floors are harder to plan.
  • Count the windows and their orientation.
  • Confirm AC capacity and zoning.
  • Ask about power backup and IT riser access.
  • Check washroom capacity for your headcount.

Next steps

If you are planning a new or improved office, start by writing down what you want to change, what must stay and the date you need the space ready. Take photos of the space as it is today and collect a few images of interiors you like.

Ask your team what helps and hinders their work today. A short survey about noise, meeting rooms and storage gives the designer a strong brief.

When you are ready, see our office interior design page or book a site visit or studio meeting. A designer can review your space, talk through options and prepare a written, itemised quote.

How to avoid these mistakes

  1. Survey how teams actually work.
  2. Plan zones before desks.
  3. Test the layout in 3D.
  4. Specify acoustics, power and light early.
  5. Leave room for growth.
